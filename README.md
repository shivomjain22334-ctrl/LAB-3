## Day 3 Lab Description

Day 3 of the Generative AI for Business course focused on understanding AI hallucination and guesswork. The main objective of the lab was to learn how vague prompts can cause Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini to assume, invent or provide unsupported information. This is especially important in aviation because incorrect information can create risks for passengers and airline operations.

In the first activity, the same vague prompt was given to ChatGPT and Gemini to prepare a passenger announcement for a delayed flight. The prompt did not provide important details such as the airline name, flight route, original and revised departure times, delay duration, reason for delay, gate number, passenger instructions or compensation information. This helped demonstrate how AI may fill missing information with assumptions instead of verified facts.

The lab then introduced a hallucination audit to identify information that was invented or assumed by the AI. I learned that a professionally written response is not automatically factually correct. AI-generated information should therefore be checked before it is used for business or passenger communication.

An improved prompt was then created by providing specific flight details and clear instructions. The prompt also instructed the AI not to invent gate, compensation, refund, meal, hotel or policy information. A verification instruction such as “[VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SYSTEM]” was also introduced. This showed how adding context, constraints and verification instructions can make AI outputs safer and more reliable.

The lab also included a fictional airline policy test involving compensation and hotel benefits. This demonstrated why official policy documents are important when asking AI about airline rules or passenger entitlements.

Overall, Day 3 taught me that AI should not be blindly trusted. Clear prompts, verified information, official sources and human judgement are necessary when using Generative AI in aviation management.
